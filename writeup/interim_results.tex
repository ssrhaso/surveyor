\begin{abstract}
We study the FF-JEPA hierarchical latent planner (frozen LeWM encoder, diffusion
subgoal drafter ``GDM'', CEM controller) on PushT and identify \emph{subgoal scale}
--- the spacing $S$ between drafted subgoals, with the control loop matched to it ---
as the dominant unexplored lever. Success rate (SR) over stride is non-monotone with a
clear interior optimum at $S{=}10$: \ours{97.2\%/96.4\%} (\degc{20}/\degc{5} criteria)
versus $91.1\%/86.3\%$ for the paper's native $S{=}25$ configuration under an
identical protocol. On the paper's own published protocols the winning configuration
\ours{beats the published numbers at both horizons} ($96.1 \to 98.4$ at $t{=}25$,
$91.8 \to 97.9$ at $t{=}75$, \degc{20}, $n{=}256$); on the paper's hardest setting
(random initialization, published $82.4\%$, reproduced baseline $78.1\%$ strict) it
records \ours{zero failures in 1024 episodes across four seeds} (SR $\ge 99.7\%$,
95\% CI); and on a fixed-population horizon sweep $t \in \{25,\dots,100\}$ the
baseline decays monotonically while $S{=}10$ stays flat --- \ours{the gap widens
from $+3.3$pp to $+11.9$pp} (\degc{5}). A cadence-isolation control shows the
mechanism is \emph{scale matching}, not re-planning frequency. Separately, we port
the one component of DSpark-style speculative decoding that survives measurement ---
the draft--verify--accept skeleton, with the learned verifier replaced by the
\emph{achieved environment state} --- and show it cuts diffusion compute by
${\sim}11\times$ relative to every-step drafting and $4.4\times$ relative to the
published baseline configuration while remaining SR-neutral at the official
criterion (at 8-seed power it trades ${\sim}3.5$pp at the strict criterion at
$S{=}10$, and is SR-\emph{positive} on both criteria at $S{=}5$). Refinement heads,
learned confidence scheduling, blind multi-step commitment, and a deterministic
regressor that dominates the drafter offline --- each was implemented and measurably
\emph{hurt} in closed loop; we report them as negative results. Failure anatomy over
all residual errors shows only tolerance-boundary near-misses: the benchmark's
stated failure mode (unreachable subgoals) is eliminated at fine scale.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and faithful reproduction}

FF-JEPA (Masip et al.) plans in the latent space of a frozen LeWM JEPA world model:
a diffusion model $G$ (``GDM'': 3-block DiT, $N{=}3$ future subgoals, trained on
success-filtered expert demonstrations) proposes the next subgoal latent
$\hat z_{sg,m+1}$ from the current encoded observation; CEM then optimizes actions
against a terminal-$L_2$ cost to that subgoal, re-planning every $H$ environment
steps. The paper fixes the subgoal stride to $H{=}25$.

\paragraph{Reproduction and audit (closed).} Our faithful retraining of the GDM
reproduces the paper: short-horizon $95.31\%$ (paper $96.09\%$, same-set oracle
$93.36\%$, $n{=}256$), long-horizon $t{=}75$ multiseed mean $90.0\%$ (paper
$91.80\%$), random-init $96.5\%/78.1\%$ at \degc{20}/\degc{5} (paper $82.42\%$).
An audit established that the environment's coded success criterion is
\degc{20} angular tolerance (the paper's \degc{5} appears in prose only); we
therefore report both tolerances throughout, treating \degc{5} as the strict
\emph{precision} criterion. Pipeline faithfulness is over-determined: a from-scratch
rebuild of the dense training set reproduces the original training population to the
episode (9{,}094) and iteration (171{,}940), and the retrained $S{=}25$ drafter
reproduces the reference offline fidelity anchor to four decimal places
($0.1593$ vs.\ $0.1593$ at $m{+}1$).

\paragraph{Protocols.} Two evaluation protocols appear below. \emph{Paper protocol}:
$n{=}256$, paper-matched sampling (used for reproduction and random-init).
\emph{Controlled protocol}: $n{=}62$ fixed held-out episodes, goal offset $150$,
budget $300$ steps, block-only criterion, CEM seeds 42--45 (curve: 42--49); every
arm sees byte-identical episodes and seeds, so within-table deltas are exact
treatment effects. Per-arm SE $\approx 1.7$pp (4 seeds) / $0.9$pp (8 seeds).

\section{The subgoal-scale law (main SR result)}

We retrain the drafter at $S \in \{5,10,15,25\}$ with the identical recipe (only the
target spacing changes; pair counts are construction-identical) and scale the control
loop with the stride (CEM horizon = re-plan cadence = $S$). Offline pre-gates
confirmed fine-stride targets are well-posed (displacement $4.7\times$ the encoder
noise floor at $S{=}5$) and that the fine-scale CEM cost landscape \emph{sharpens}
(true-plan ranking percentile $0.93$ at $S{=}5$ vs.\ $0.82$ at $S{=}25$, cost
dynamic range $3\times$ larger).

\paragraph{Mechanism: scale matching, not cadence.} A cadence-isolation control
(re-anchor every 5 steps against unchanged 25-step targets) \emph{collapses} to
$78.6\%/77.8\%$ ($-12.5$pp below the baseline): each re-draft re-samples a distant
subgoal from a wide conditional posterior, and CEM chases a churning target. Together
with the monotone commitment losses of Section~2, the correct statement is:
\textbf{closed-loop SR is governed by matching the control loop to the subgoal
scale; finer matched scales win (to an interior optimum)} --- not ``re-plan more''
and not ``re-plan less.''

\paragraph{Prediction error is horizon-intrinsic.} Across all four drafters, offline
prediction error collapses onto the \emph{absolute} look-ahead horizon, independent
of block position ($+10$ steps: $0.105$ vs.\ $0.112$; $+15$: $0.132$ vs.\ $0.140$),
and the $S{=}5$ drafter's \emph{third} block position ($+15$ steps, $0.140$)
outpredicts the $S{=}25$ drafter's \emph{first} ($+25$, $0.159$). ``Suffix decay''
is task-future uncertainty, not an architectural defect --- retiring intra-block
architecture work.

\section{Reality-verified speculative consumption (main efficiency result)}

The surviving DSpark idea is its \emph{skeleton}: draft a block cheaply, verify,
accept the surviving prefix. Our port replaces the learned verifier with reality:
draft the $N$-block once; after each hop compare the \emph{achieved} latent to the
subgoal just pursued; within tolerance $\tau$ $\Rightarrow$ consume the next drafted
position (no diffusion call); otherwise $\Rightarrow$ re-draft from the achieved
state. Re-anchoring is never sacrificed when reality diverges. Offline simulators
calibrated $\tau$ and showed 8-step DDIM drafts are indistinguishable from 50-step
drafts (matched-noise distance $0.03$; spread preserved); both transferred to the
closed loop.

\section{Is the diffusion drafter necessary? (resolved)}

The last open question: a deterministic MLP regressor ($z_{cond} \to$ block,
the offline-strong JointMLP class, wd $10^{-3}$) as the subgoal source,
re-anchoring every replan --- offline it matches the diffusion drafter's
prediction error on the same validation windows ($0.30$ vs.\ $0.32$ at $S{=}10$).
Closed loop, seeds 42--45:

\caption{Diffusion vs.\ deterministic regression as the subgoal source.
\textbf{The drafter's stochasticity is load-bearing}: at matched-or-better
offline fidelity, the zero-spread mean target loses everywhere --- and its
$-11.7$pp strict-criterion loss at $S{=}25$ quantitatively mirrors the
refinement head's $-11$pp (Section 2), closing the spread thesis causally:
any operator that regresses subgoals toward the conditional mean, whether by
refinement or by architecture, damages control. The gap narrows at finer
scale (closer targets, near-unimodal posterior) but never closes.}
\end{table}

\section{Failure anatomy: what is left}

Instrumented traces (every re-plan's achieved latent and drafted subgoal) over all
8 seeds of $S{=}10$: \textbf{18 failures in 496 episodes, all tolerance-boundary
near-misses} --- final latent distance-to-goal within the \emph{success}
distribution's 95th percentile (one failure sits \emph{closer} than the median
success: a pure angle miss). Zero unreachable-subgoal failures, zero divergence,
zero stuck episodes; successful episodes' commanded-to-achieved step ratio has
median $1.06$. FF-JEPA's own reported failure mode (``subgoal looks right, agent
cannot reach it'') is eliminated at fine scale; the residue is quantization at the
success threshold.
